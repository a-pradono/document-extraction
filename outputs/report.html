Receipt & Invoice Extraction — End-to-End Report

Status, 18 September 2026. The system is built and tested (435 unit tests, offline end-to-end 7/7). Ground truth is fully verified — 60 of 60 documents checked against their images by a human, a pass that corrected five money-field errors (§2.4). The confidence model is fitted on a real run of the calibration split (§4.2). The test split has not been run yet, so there is still no accuracy scorecard: §4.5 is empty and says so. Nothing in §4 is a placeholder — every table is either a measurement or explicitly marked not-yet-run.

1. Introduction

The problem

A client needs structured data pulled out of a stack of real documents — scanned, photographed, multi-page, inconsistently laid out. Some fields are simple lookups (a merchant name, a date). Others require reasoning across sections: a total that must reconcile against line items and tax, a party's role in a transaction, a table of line items that spans a page break.

What this system is optimized for

Not a demo that works on a clean PDF. The design target is a system whose accuracy can be proven, that knows when it is unsure, and whose quality, latency and cost can be reasoned about. Three properties drove every decision:

  1. It must not hallucinate. A field absent from the document returns null. No value is invented, and every value kept is traceable to text found in the document.
  2. It must abstain rather than guess. Per-field confidence is computed and fields below a fitted threshold are withheld at the API boundary rather than asserted.
  3. Evaluation is the primary deliverable. The harness was built before any prompt tuning, so every change is judged by re-running a scorecard. Section 4.3 shows this paying off: the harness caught three defects in its own ground truth and one in itself, each of which would have produced confident, wrong numbers.

2. Data

60 documents, three sources, two document types, four languages (Indonesian, English, Spanish, French/Dutch), three independent label schemas.

2.1 Why receipts as the primary type

Receipts exercise the hardest requirement — verifiable cross-section reasoning — better than any alternative at this scale. A receipt carries a line-item table, a subtotal, a tax amount and a total, and those must reconcile. That gives a model-free ground-truth signal: subtotal + service − discount + tax == total either holds or it does not, whatever any model claims. That property is the foundation of the confidence design in §3.3.

The trade-off, stated plainly: receipts are natively single-page with one counterparty, so they cannot exercise multi-page reading or party-role resolution. Hence invoices.

2.2 Receipt sources (50 documents)

Source Docs Role Labels
CORD v2 (naver-clova-ix/cord-v2, CC BY 4.0) 25 Structured backbone — line items with quantity and unit price, subtotal → total Script-converted from the dataset, header fields hand-drafted from the images
WildReceipt (MMOCR, Apache-2.0) 25 Template diversity — photographed in the wild, unseen templates Script-converted, re-spaced and extended by hand

Small and trustworthy over large and noisy. Trustworthy ground truth is the highest-weighted evaluation criterion, and a larger set with unverified labels produces metrics that cannot be trusted — while costing proportionally more per eval run.

Stratified sampling, not first-N. CORD holds 1,000 receipts; taking the first 25 would produce an accidentally easy set. Sampling is stratified by line-item count and spread across merchants, with the chosen ids pinned so a converter fix re-converts the same documents rather than reshuffling the splits.

CORD is anonymised at source. The dataset blurs the merchant header on nearly every receipt, so most CORD merchant, address and date labels are explicit null — which is itself a useful annotation: it turns "did the model invent a merchant?" into a scorable question. Four headers remain legible through the blur (PIZZA HUT, Solaria, XXI Cafe, and a Korean logo) and are labelled per the rule label what a human can read.

2.3 Invoices (10 documents)

Added to cover the two requirements receipts structurally cannot: multi-page reading and party-role resolution (vendor vs customer, where a swap surfaces as the swapped_field error label).

Source: RVL-CDIP-N multipage (jordyvl/rvl_cdip_n_mp, invoice class; Larson et al., NeurIPS 2022 Datasets & Benchmarks; CC BY-NC 4.0, non-commercial). Ten real invoices, 1–5 pages each, obtained through public-records requests: a police department, two TV stations, a school district, law firms, a state agency, small manufacturers. scripts/fetch_invoices.py streams the archive and extracts only the ten pinned files, each verified by SHA-256.

A synthetic invoice set was evaluated and rejected. The candidate was one template filled with Faker data: totals that did not reconcile, and every document a single page. It could not test either property invoices were added for. The rejection is recorded in docs/DATASETS.md with the evidence.

Real documents bring real mess, all recorded in _provenance.json:

  • inv_0004 prints an arithmetic error — rows sum to 56.56 against a printed 36.59. Kept as-is: a document whose printed total is genuinely wrong is a valuable test case.
  • inv_0005 has a row struck through by hand with "duplicate invoicing" written beside it, while the printed total still includes it. Labelled as printed.
  • inv_0006 is three documents in one PDF (two invoices and a credit memo). Flagged as a replacement candidate; the "one document" premise is contestable there.
  • inv_0002 is the sharpest role trap in the set: the customer is the billing agency, not the advertiser whose campaign it is.
  • inv_0010 runs 225 line items across 5 pages; the transcribed rows sum exactly to the printed $59,403.38.

n=10 is deliberately small: enough to prove the system generalises to a second document type, not enough to pretend invoices are a co-equal dataset. Invoice results are reported as a separate breakdown, never pooled into the receipt headline, and the confidence model is fitted on receipts only — invoice confidence is uncalibrated.

Privacy and licence. These are real public records naming real people and organisations, under a non-commercial licence. The repository is private; published views carry the dataset attribution.

2.4 Verification — what the human pass actually found

60 of 60 documents verified. Every label was checked against its image, and the pass found five money-field errors, all in WildReceipt, none of which the model-free audit could resolve on its own. The audit could say this does not reconcile; only a human could say what the right number was.

Document Defect After correction
wild_0003 Subtotal 675.00 for a printed Rs1,675.00; one of two printed taxes (VAT 13.13%, Service TAX 4.94%) dropped 1,675.00 + 82.75 + 213.36 = 1,971.11, the printed Cash amount
wild_0019 Subtotal 97.30 for a printed 87.30; the printed 10% service charge missing entirely 87.30 + 8.73 + 6.72 = 102.75, the printed TOTAL
wild_0018 A row annotated 5390 for a printed 5.90 — a defect in the published dataset, not in our conversion 6 rows sum to exactly 47.30, the printed SUBTTL
wild_0015 Three printed coupons (−0.75, −0.25, −1.00) dropped, so rows summed to 5.00 against a printed total of 3.00 5.00 − 2.00 = 3.00
wild_0004 One fuel purchase split across three junk rows: REGULAR/15863, a bare 3479, a bare 55.19 — gallons, price per gallon and fuel total 15.863 gal × $3.479 = $55.19, plus $10.00 = $65.19

That is a 10% error rate in the money fields of one source, and every one of those errors would have been charged to the model: a correct extraction scored as wrong_value on exactly the fields the confidence design leans on hardest. It is the empirical case for "small and verified" over "large and noisy", and the reason this project audits its ground truth rather than assuming it.

Judgement calls are recorded per document in _provenance.json, including those the drafter could not resolve and the reviewer settled: ambiguous $ currencies (AUD vs SGD vs USD, inferred from tax registrations), day-first vs month-first dates, and cord_0019, where the merchant name is blurred by the dataset's own anonymisation.

2.5 No own scans — and the compensation

None were available, so that bonus is forfeited. Two substitutes:

  1. Source diversity — three sources, two document types, four languages, three independent label schemas. Per-source breakdowns test generalisation in a way one dataset cannot.
  2. Synthetic degradation — 25 degraded copies (blur, rotation, JPEG, fade) scored against the unchanged ground truth. Clearly labelled synthetic, excluded from the headline scorecard structurally rather than by convention.

3. Method

3.1 Architecture

image          ──► Model A (VLM)  ──► { transcription, document, _confidence }
transcription  ──► Model B (LLM)  ──► document  (independent re-extraction)
                   Python validation (arithmetic / format — model-free)
                                   ──► per-field confidence ──► abstention
Role Model Why
Model A — VLM reader gemini-3.6-flash Layout is the problem: "which of these five numbers is the total" is answered by position, which flat OCR destroys
Model B — LLM reasoner nvidia/nemotron-3-super-120b-a12b:free Cross-section reasoning and normalisation, text-only and cheap. Different family on purpose
Cascade first pass gemini-3.5-flash-lite Cheap read; escalates to the full pipeline on doubt (§4.5)

Different families, deliberately. Sibling models fail identically on the same smudged digit, which would inflate the agreement signal exactly where it must be honest.

The transcription replaces a separate OCR stage and serves as the groundedness source, so the anti-hallucination signal survives without a local OCR dependency. A PaddleOCR path was built and abandoned: too slow on a CPU-only machine.

Multi-page documents are rendered page by page and sent in one call, so the model sees the whole document; MAX_PAGES (5) refuses anything longer rather than silently reading part of it.

3.2 Typed, validated output

Model output is parsed into Pydantic models (Receipt, Invoice, sharing LineItem). Money is Decimal, never float, and crosses the JSON boundary as a string. Dates are YYYY-MM-DD. Absent fields are null — never an empty string, a zero or a plausible guess. Malformed JSON gets one corrective retry showing the model its own bad output; a truncated response fails loudly as TruncatedResponse rather than as a JSON parse error; per-field salvage keeps the fields that did parse.

Implementation note: Receipt.date originally shadowed the datetime.date import inside its own annotation, so Pydantic resolved the type to NoneType and silently rejected every date — which would have zeroed date F1 with no visible error. Fixed and covered by a regression test. Worth recording because the failure was silent.

3.3 Confidence: four signals, fitted weights

Signal Strength What it knows
validated Strongest Deterministic arithmetic — reconciliation, rows multiply out, rows sum to subtotal, date parses, currency is ISO-4217, and for invoices due ≥ issue and vendor ≠ customer. Model-free, cannot be bluffed
grounded Strong Does the value appear in the transcription, type-aware and whole-token?
agreement Moderate Do Model A and Model B agree after normalisation? Row-level F1 for tables
self_report Weakest The model's stated certainty

Known limitation: Model B reads Model A's transcription, so they are only partially independent — a misread digit propagates and inflates agreement. This is precisely why validated exists: it is independent of the reading step. True reading independence needs a second vision model on the raw image (§5).

Weights are fitted per field group by logistic regression on the calibration split; groups with too few observations keep default weights rather than overfit. The threshold is read off a risk–coverage curve against a target precision, never hand-picked. Real fitted values are in §4.2.

3.4 Evaluation harness

Scoring runs after a normalisation layer (dates → ISO, money → Decimal, currency symbols, case and whitespace) so metrics measure semantic correctness, not string noise.

Accuracy: per-field precision / recall / F1, reported normalised and exact (the gap is format sensitivity); micro and macro F1; Document Accuracy Rate; and line-item F1 via greedy one-to-one matching on description similarity plus amount.

Error taxonomy: missing (under-extraction), spurious (hallucination), wrong_value, format_error (same content, different format — deliberately not counted as wrong) and swapped_field (the value belongs to another field, e.g. vendor ↔ customer).

Confidence quality: AUC for discrimination, ECE for calibration, plus a reliability diagram and risk–coverage curve. The two have different fixes, and the scorecard prints the diagnosis: AUC ≈ 0.5 means the signals must change; good AUC with high ECE means refit.

Two rules the harness enforces about its own inputs (both added after they bit — see §4.3):

  • A field with no label is not scored, and the scorecard reports how many were skipped. A null label means "not on the document"; an unlabelled field is a different thing, and conflating them scores a correct reading as a hallucination.
  • Calibration refuses to fit on less than 80% of the split and writes nothing, rather than producing a threshold from whatever documents ran before a quota expired.

3.5 Deployment and the CPU constraint

No GPU on the target machine, which decided where each model runs: both models hosted (a 3B model generating ~600 tokens of JSON on CPU takes 40–90 s/doc against 3–8 s hosted), with validation, groundedness and metrics local, deterministic and free. All model access goes through one OpenAI-compatible provider abstraction, so endpoints are swappable via .env with no code change — which is what makes a local open-weights VLM a config change given a GPU.

Caching by input + model + prompt hash keeps API calls to one per document across repeated eval runs. Re-running the harness after a metrics change costs zero calls.

Measured free-tier limits (not documentation, observation): Google's free tier allows 20 requests per model per day — not the 250 the docs suggested — which stopped a calibration run at 8 of 18 documents. Billing was enabled to lift it. OpenRouter's free tier allows 50 requests per day and capped a later run.

3.6 Production surface

docker compose up serves the API; docker compose run --rm eval waits for the healthcheck and runs the harness against that running service, so the harness scores exactly the code the API runs.

Endpoints: GET /health, GET /metrics, POST /extract, POST /extract/batch with bounded concurrency (free tiers rate-limit; unbounded fan-out only collects 429s). POST /extract takes a document_type of receipt or invoice and returns 400 for anything else.

The output contract is documented and tested (docs/OUTPUT.md): document, confidence, abstained, withheld, signals, validation, validation_notes, transcription, usage. Abstention is applied at the boundary — the value is removed from document and kept under withheld, so a consumer never reads an unasserted value while a human reviewer can still see what was read. A test fails if the documented payload drifts from the code.

Resilience: a failed document returns 200 with a populated error and is scored as an empty extraction — a recall failure, never a silent drop from the denominator.

Regression gate: exits 0 (ship), 1 (regression) or 2 (invalid comparison). It fails on any new hallucination, and on an F1 drop only when at least two documents flipped — at this size a threshold alone cries wolf. Runs are fingerprinted (labels, documents, prompts, models, threshold), and it refuses to compare across a label change, because the delta would measure the relabelling.

4.2 Confidence quality — measured

Run: 18 calibration receipts (invoices excluded from the fit), 198 field observations, 100% of the split extracted.

Metric Value Target
AUC (discrimination, in-sample) 0.845 > 0.75
ECE (calibration, in-sample) 0.033 < 0.10

Fitted weights per field group — a finding in their own right:

Group n agreement grounded validated self_report bias
string 54 +0.85 +0.81 +0.35 −0.85 +1.12
money 90 +0.81 +0.82 +0.27 +0.11 +1.16
date 18 +0.33 −0.02 −0.02 −0.64 +2.04
currency 18 +1.53 +1.89 −0.46 −0.69 +0.16
table 18 −0.36 −0.15 +1.11 +0.65 −0.47

Three things worth stating plainly:

  1. self_report is negative in four of five groups. The model's own stated confidence is anti-informative: when it says it is sure, it is likelier to be wrong. This is the argument for a multi-signal design rather than trusting a model's self-assessment, and it is a measurement, not a prior.
  2. validated dominates the table group (+1.11) while contributing modestly elsewhere — exactly as designed. The arithmetic check is the signal that cannot be bluffed, and a table is where it has the most to say.
  3. grounded carries currency (+1.89), because the failure mode there is a model inferring a currency the document never prints.

Operating point, read off the risk–coverage curve:

At threshold 0.875 the system keeps 70.7% of fields at 95.0% precision.

That is the honest trade-off: reaching 95% precision costs abstention on 29% of fields. Set CONFIDENCE_THRESHOLD=0.875 in .env to use it.

In-sample, on 18 documents. These numbers set the threshold; they are not the reported result. The reported result comes from --set test, which has not been run.

4.3 What the harness caught in its own ground truth

The evaluation found four defects that would each have produced confident, wrong numbers. They are reported because the catching is the point.

Defect Symptom Fix
Unlabelled fields scored as absent merchant_name had no label on 48 of 60 documents; a correct reading counted as spurious — a hallucination — and taught the confidence model that a visible, grounded string is probably wrong Fields with no annotation are excluded from scoring and reported as a gap
WildReceipt strips spaces (TUSCANYRISTORANTE) Correct readings scored as wrong_value Whitespace-insensitive string comparison, documented as a deliberate lenience
Partial calibration accepted silently A rate-limited run fitted weights on 8 of 18 documents, wrote a threshold and exited 0 — the report even recorded n_documents: 18, the count it asked for rather than the count it saw Refuses to fit below 80% coverage; records real coverage and every failure
Scorecard written in the platform default encoding Em dashes became undecodable bytes in a file declaring UTF-8 Explicit UTF-8 on every write

The evidence for the first two, measured on the calibration split before the fix: string fields that were grounded scored 15% correct, against 93% for ungrounded ones. Grounding was working perfectly and the scorer was calling it wrong. Fixing it moved the grounded weight for strings from −0.95 (anti-informative) to +0.81, and let date and currency be fitted at all.

It also made the headline AUC fall from 0.917 to 0.845 — the earlier number was largely the model learning to predict broken labels, a leaked feature. About 44 of the 59 "errors" it had been trained to detect were not errors.

4.8 Statistical honesty

  • DAR is binary per document, so at n=39 in test its 95% CI is roughly ±8pp. Two versions differing by 3pp are not distinguishable.
  • Per-field F1 is more stable (several hundred field-level judgments), which is why the regression gate rests on per-field metrics rather than DAR.
  • Invoices at n=10 are indicative only, and their confidence is uncalibrated: the weights were fitted on receipts, and 10 documents cannot support their own fit.
  • Ground truth is single-annotator and, at present, mostly unverified. No inter-annotator agreement has been measured, so label noise is unquantified.
  • The calibration numbers in §4.2 are in-sample on 18 documents. They set the operating point; they do not demonstrate it.
